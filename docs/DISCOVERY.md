<a id="overview"></a>

## ✦ Overview

Discovery is one of the central ideas behind The Gaming Horizon.

Players often know they want to play something.

They do not always know:

```text
WHAT GAME?

WHAT EXPERIENCE?

WHAT COMMUNITY?

WHAT CREATOR?

WHAT COMPETITION?

WHAT PROJECT?

WHAT COMES NEXT?
```

Discovery exists to reduce the distance between:

```text
"I WANT SOMETHING TO PLAY"
```

and:

```text
"I FOUND SOMETHING WORTH EXPLORING"
```

---

## ✦ Discovery Vision

The goal is not to create the largest possible catalog.

The goal is to make relevant experiences easier to find and understand.

```text
PLAYER CURIOSITY
       │
       ▼
    DISCOVERY
       │
       ▼
     CONTEXT
       │
       ▼
     CHOICE
       │
       ▼
   EXPERIENCE
       │
       ▼
NEW INTEREST
       │
       ▼
NEW DISCOVERY
```

Discovery should become a loop rather than a dead end.

---

## ✦ Discovery in One Sentence

> **Help players find meaningful gaming experiences without making discovery feel like work.**

<a id="discovery-principles"></a>

## ✦ Discovery Principles

### `01` — Player Intent First

Discovery should begin with what the player is trying to find.

---

### `02` — Relevance Before Volume

More results are not necessarily better results.

---

### `03` — Context Before Clicks

Players should receive enough information to make meaningful choices.

---

### `04` — Exploration Before Manipulation

Discovery should encourage curiosity without using deceptive engagement
patterns.

---

### `05` — Search and Browse Together

Some users know what they want.

Others want to explore.

Both should be supported.

---

### `06` — Personalization With Control

Personalization should improve relevance without requiring unnecessary data.

---

### `07` — Community as Context

People can help people discover games.

Community knowledge can complement algorithms.

---

### `08` — Creators as Discovery Guides

Creators can add useful explanation, context, and perspective.

---

### `09` — Accessibility by Default

Discovery must remain usable across devices and access needs.

---

### `10` — AI as Assistance

AI may help interpret intent, but user choice remains central.

---

### `11` — Transparency Before Hidden Influence

Users should not be misled about why something is being shown.

---

### `12` — Discovery Should Evolve

The best discovery model today may not be the best one tomorrow.


---

## ✦ Search Quality

Search quality should not be judged only through result count.

A useful search experience should ask:

```text
DID THE USER FIND SOMETHING RELEVANT?

WAS THE RESULT UNDERSTANDABLE?

COULD THEY REFINE THEIR SEARCH?

WERE EMPTY STATES HELPFUL?

WERE RESULTS ACCESSIBLE?
```

---

## ✦ Empty Search States

A search returning no result should still provide direction.

Avoid:

```text
NO RESULTS
```

and nothing else.

Prefer:

```text
NO RESULTS

TRY:
- another title
- fewer terms
- another category
- browse discovery
```

where relevant.

```text

TOO MANY COMPETING ACTIONS
```

The card should help users decide whether to explore further.

```text

NEW COMMUNITIES

NEW CREATORS

NEW EXPERIENCES
```

## ✦ Exploration vs Personalization

```text
PERSONALIZATION
      │
      └── More relevant to known interests
```

```text
EXPLORATION
      │
      └── Introduces useful new possibilities
```

A strong discovery system may need both.

## ✦ Personalization

Personalization should remain proportionate.

Preferred model:

```text
USER-CHOSEN PREFERENCES
          +
CURRENT CONTEXT
          +
MINIMUM NECESSARY DATA
          +
USER CONTROL
          =
RESPONSIBLE PERSONALIZATION
```

---

## ✦ Personalization Should Not Mean Surveillance

Avoid:

```text
COLLECT EVERYTHING
       │
       ▼
BUILD PROFILE
       │
       ▼
MAYBE FIND A USE LATER
```

Prefer:

```text
DEFINE DISCOVERY NEED
       │
       ▼
IDENTIFY MINIMUM SIGNAL
       │
       ▼
USE
       │
       ▼
DELIVER USER VALUE
```

<a id="privacy"></a>

## ✦ Privacy

Discovery systems can become data-intensive if poorly designed.

Before using a signal:

```text
WHY IS IT NEEDED?

IS IT NECESSARY?

CAN THE USER CONTROL IT?

CAN LESS DATA WORK?

IS IT SENSITIVE?

HOW LONG IS IT REQUIRED?

IS IT SHARED EXTERNALLY?
```

---

## ✦ Discovery Data Minimization

```text
POSSIBLE SIGNAL
      │
      ▼
NEEDED FOR DISCOVERY?
  ┌───┴────┐
  ▼        ▼
```

## ✦ Discovery Golden Rules

### `01`

**Help the player find something meaningful.**

### `02`

**Relevance matters more than result count.**

### `03`

**Search and exploration should coexist.**

### `04`

**Do not mistake popularity for relevance.**

### `05`

**Personalization should not require unnecessary surveillance.**

### `06`

**AI should understand intent, not invent results.**

### `07`

**Accessibility belongs throughout the discovery journey.**

### `08`

**Explain important discovery context where practical.**

### `09`

**Never invent ratings, rankings, availability, game counts, or recommendation metrics.**

### `10`

**Every discovery should leave room for another horizon.**

---

## ✦ Discovery Quality Standard

A Gaming Horizon discovery experience should aim to be:

| # | Standard | Meaning |
|:---:|---|---|
| `01` | **Relevant** | Results relate meaningfully to user intent |
| `02` | **Explorable** | Users can discover without knowing exact terms |
| `03` | **Clear** | Results provide useful context |
| `04` | **Fast** | Discovery responds without unnecessary delay |
| `05` | **Accessible** | Search and browsing reduce participation barriers |
| `06` | **Controllable** | Users can refine and reject suggestions |
| `07` | **Private** | Personalization uses proportionate data |
| `08` | **Secure** | Inputs and external data are handled safely |
| `09` | **Truthful** | Information is not fabricated |
| `10` | **Connected** | Discovery links ecosystem experiences |
| `11` | **Resilient** | Alternative paths exist when systems fail |
| `12` | **Evolvable** | New discovery models can emerge responsibly |
